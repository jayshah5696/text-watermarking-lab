One complete worked choice

How a key changes the next token.

We will follow recorded position 4 from its recent history to one chosen token. Every input, score change, probability, interval, and checker step stays visible.

The short answer: the recent IDs and a public teaching key select five of 20 candidates. Those five scores receive +2. All 20 probabilities are recalculated, and one draw still decides the token. The rule changes chances. It does not force a result.

What this page uses
20 synthetic options. No model. No tokenizer.

The word labels make IDs readable. They are not output from a language model. The trace teaches a KGW-style idea with its own toy selector. It does not reproduce Anthropic's private implementation.

Each candidate has an ID, a readable label, and a raw logit. A logit is an unrestricted score. A larger logit receives more probability after the complete score list is converted.

The checker repeats the selector on observed history.

Selector and relative-odds notes

The exact toy message format is:

lab-02|v1|<public-key>|<comma-separated-context-ids>|<candidate-id>

For one green token compared with one unchanged red token, adding 2 multiplies their probability ratio by exp(2) ≈ 7.389. It does not multiply the green token's final probability by 7.389 because the shared denominator also changes.

This selector is a teaching rule. It is not an upstream KGW pseudorandom function and must not be presented as compatible with one.
