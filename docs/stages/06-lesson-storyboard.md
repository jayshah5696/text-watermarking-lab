# Stage 6 lesson storyboard

## One question

What scores does the Stage 5 checker assign to text that was not generated with its key?

## Story continuity

Open on the Stage 5 result `0 of 12 controls above z > 3`. Keep its green hits, eligible count, z,
and cutoff on screen. Twelve purpose-written prompts were enough to demonstrate code, not to learn
the background score distribution. Replace the generator with a pinned box of web documents while
the checker stays unchanged.

## Beat order

1. Recall one narrow Stage 5 fact: no control in the 12-row ladder crossed the frozen cutoff.
2. Ask why 12 cannot tell us how often unmarked outside text looks unusual.
3. Define a negative reference as text selected without using the checker result.
4. Open one C4 row as metadata plus a short excerpt. Call it natural web, not verified human text.
5. Walk the row through each deterministic filter. Reveal one reason at a time.
6. Tokenize the accepted row. Keep one 500-token strip visible.
7. Mark tokens 0 through 49 as the future prompt and 50 through 449 as today's continuation.
8. Replay the same keyed green checks from Stage 5 on the 400-token continuation.
9. Count `G/T`. Show expected hits and ordinary movement as concrete numbers before the z formula.
10. Place the row against the unchanged cutoff. Interpret the result narrowly.
11. Reveal 12, then 100, then all 1,000 recorded scores. Do not resample.
12. Label the maximum-z row and keep it visible. Explain a natural-web cutoff crossing as an
    empirical false alarm for this declared negative cohort, not proof of text origin.
13. Show the observed fraction as a count first. Then show why 1,000 rows cannot test one-in-100,000.
14. Compare all adjacent pairs with distinct value pairs for the same text. Reconnect to Stage 4's
    repetition mismatch.
15. Show the selection funnel and the next 24 frozen rows. Stage 7 may use them; Stage 6 does not.
16. End with the exact claim and transition into paired generation.

## Guided interactions

### Inspect one row

- Instruction: advance from source identity to filter, token split, keyed checks, and score.
- Fixed: row, tokenizer, key, profile, and cutoff.
- Changed: visible processing layer.
- Watch: no score participates in row selection.

### Reveal the cohort

- Instruction: show 12, 100, then 1,000 recorded rows.
- Fixed: ordered artifact and cutoff.
- Changed: number of visible observations.
- Watch: the maximum and observed crossing count can change as evidence accumulates.

### Compare counting rules

- Instruction: switch from every adjacent pair to each distinct value pair once.
- Fixed: exact copied token sequence and keyed membership.
- Changed: whether a repeated pair supplies another count.
- Watch: `T`, `G`, and z may change together.

## Screenshot tests

1. A newcomer can explain why the 50-token prefix is not scored in Stage 6.
2. A newcomer can identify every dot as a recorded natural-web continuation and read the cutoff as
   a separate rule.
3. A newcomer can state the observed fraction as `count / 1000` and explain why it is not a
   production guarantee.
