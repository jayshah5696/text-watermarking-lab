# Article opening storyboard

The opening uses one continuous object: a yes-or-no mark. It begins as visible slack in media, becomes a weighted coin, becomes the `32/80` calculation, then maps to one token choice.

1. **Opening question.** Put the reader's confusion on the page: an image can hide tiny pixel changes, but what can plain text change without changing what it says?
2. **Media comparison.** Show image, video, and text as increasingly constrained objects. Attribute the framing to Theo. Do not discuss dates or Claude's implementation.
3. **One-sentence mechanism.** During generation, slightly favor some acceptable choices. During checking, count those choices.
4. **Two weighted coins.** Show a 25 percent baseline and 40 percent nudge. Let the reader rerun both 40-flip sequences. State that these browser draws are illustrations.
5. **One recorded-style calculation.** Keep `32 heads in 80 flips` fixed. Reveal `20`, `12`, `3.87`, and `3.10` one line at a time.
6. **Ordinary movement.** Show 20 repeatable illustrative baseline batches to make variation visible before naming standard deviation.
7. **Name z and the cutoff.** Place 20, 24, 28, and 32 heads on one line. Read z as a sentence. Then show the separate cutoff and exact upper tail.
8. **Change length only.** Keep both probabilities and the cutoff fixed. Compare persistent excess with baseline movement at 40, 80, 160, 200, and 400.
9. **Repeat whole batches.** Draw two analytic binomial hills for 40, 80, or 400 and let the reader move the cutoff after guided presets. Show false alarms and misses.
10. **Map coin to token.** Preserve the visual identity of one flip and turn it into one next-token choice. This is the handoff to the keyed vocabulary section.
11. **Show measured Stage 1 evidence.** End the opening experiment with all five recorded rates and the exact caveat.
12. **Show the code.** Present the actual scorer, simulator, and execution loop in reading order. Keep configuration and reproduction behind a disclosure.
13. **Transition.** The coin explains accumulation but has no secret rule. The next section gives it a key.
